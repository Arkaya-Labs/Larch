## Summary

The goal was larch ahead of llama.cpp outside noise at W4@1, @4, @8 and @16, against the protocol's `--n-cpu-moe 3` with
4 beside it. **It is met at W4@1 only, and missed at W4@4 and W4@8.** At W4@16 larch is ahead of the fewest llama.cpp
setting that fits its free memory, but llama.cpp's faster settings there were not run, so W4@16 does not count as met.

One rule for llama.cpp's rows at every load: the protocol's `--n-cpu-moe 3` leads and `--n-cpu-moe 4` sits beside it,
however far past its reported free memory each loads. The fewest setting that fits that memory is beside them where it
ran: ncm 4 at W4@1 (also the beside row) and ncm 9 at W4@16. By the load lines' arithmetic it would be ncm 5 at W4@4 and
7 at W4@8; neither ran. At W4@16, where ncm 3 and 4 collapsed, ncm 5 to 8 were loaded but not run.

1. **W4@4: behind, −5.7 %.** 246.0 against 260.9 tok/s for `--n-cpu-moe 3`. Its lever, one host layer fewer (Lane B),
   is 263 MB short of the free memory larch plans within. llama.cpp's ncm 3 load runs 609 MiB past the free memory it
   reports. Larch is ahead of ncm 4 (+6.0 %), and on TTFT p50 and p99 (−28 % and −52 %).
2. **W4@8: behind, −19.9 % against ncm 3 and −14.8 % against ncm 4.** 246.1 against 307.2 and 288.8, one run a side.
   llama.cpp's loads ran 1,061 and 697 MiB past their free memory and seat all 8 chats. Larch seats 4, so its TTFT p50 is

## Lane B (one host layer fewer): dropped at its gate

The gate was a default load of 3 host layers with the pool on and 4 state slots, inside the free memory. A clean idle
card cannot hold that. Measured with `LARCH_CPU_EXPERT_LAYERS=3` on dev (`step0-probe-cel3-w4-4.txt`):

| 3 host layers at | Free after the load | The check's need | Short |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 state slots, pool counted | 303,149,056 B | 637.1 MB (452.6 MB + the 184.5 MB pool) | 334 MB (263 MB less the 68 MiB swing allowance) |
| 2 state slots, pool counted | 498,466,816 B | 579.0 MB | 81 MB; the load kept 2 slots and no pool |

So the load stays at 4 host layers. Of the WHY figures:

- **The pool's plan size (a): confirmed.** The plan reserved 318,767,104 B for the pool (the MTP block's Q8_0 experts plus
  the copy stream), and the load wanted 184,549,376 B. That is 134.2 MB over.
- **The "further 132-199 MB" (b): 86.6 MB, more than 20 % off, so the lane stops by the rule.** The load's measured take
  (`model_bytes`) less the terms the plan counts (weights on the device, activations, f16 routers, recurrent state) is
  86,585,216 B at 4 host layers and 87,437,184 B at 3, on readings that did not swing. The 3-layer, 4-slot reading also
  swung low by one quantum (67.4 MB), which makes 154.0 MB. That is inside WHY's 132-199 MB, and it is what `FREE_SWING`
  already allows for.

Both findings went into Lane A as its part 3: with the pool sized right and the 86.6 MB counted, the plan has room at 4
host layers for a fifth state slot. Part 3 was then dropped by the 3 % rule (Lane A).

## Lane A (8 and 16 chats): gate missed, three parts kept and one dropped

Each part was written test-first on the CPU backend:

1. **No up-switch on held seats** (`Demand::seats`, `crates/larch-engine/src/engine.rs`). A load that already holds the
   `MANY_SEATS` state slots the many-chats plan pays for is never pressed up. Its waiting requests take slots as they free
   or grow live (`grow_seats`). Test: `many_chats_on_held_seats_keep_their_load`, 8 chats on 4 slots whose turns end and
   come back, faked to the CUDA gate. It armed a switch after 12 pressed turns on dev. **Kept.**
2. **Many chats without a route histogram keep whole layers and the pool** (`crates/larch-engine/src/lib.rs`). As
   measured, the expert cache was taken only with a histogram to place experts from; without one its plan was whole
   layers with no pool. Such a load records no routes, so on its own a card never starts a histogram
   (`LARCH_EXPERT_CACHE=1` still does). After the grid the PR review's fix made the cache `LARCH_EXPERT_CACHE` only, so a
   histogram beside the model no longer picks it either (Review). Test:
   `a_many_chats_load_keeps_whole_layers_and_the_stream_pool`. **Kept.**
3. **Plan terms: the pool from the trunk's layers and the load's own take.** `stream_pool_bytes` counted only the config's
   layers, and the whole-layer plan reserved the load's 86.6 MB past its terms (`LOAD_PAST_PLAN`, 84 MiB). That let 4 host

4. **Decode buckets stop at a hybrid's state slots**, as vLLM's stop at `max_num_seqs`. On 6 slots a 5-row step pads to

## Lane E (hot experts on the device, from the shipped histogram): STOP at E1

The spike capped the expert cache's split. `fit::capped_split`, behind a temporary `LARCH_SPLIT_LAYERS=N` in
`expert_residency`, kept today's byte budget. It picked the N layers whose coldest experts, at the host's bytes over N
each, hold the fewest routes, and moved those layers' experts to the host, coldest first, until the rest fit. Every other
layer stayed whole on the device. It was test-first: `a_capped_split_hosts_the_coldest_experts_of_few_layers`, on

  `params`, from the `--n-cpu-moe` on that command line, as soon as the driver has written the file.
- **Statistics.** Each cell is the mean of its runs, and spread = (max − min) / mean. A margin is noise when under 2 % or
  under either side's spread; a one-run cell has only the 2 % rule. These are the 26 Nov rules.
