# Zoom, cache and route before pruning tokens

larch's image path is already faster than llama.cpp, and on its own 12 screenshot tasks it is about as accurate as these model sizes allow. The best remaining wins on small models and 4 GB GPUs are therefore not token pruning or new precision tricks. They are cheap engine fixes that recover accuracy the models already have. In priority order: apply the vendor's sampling settings and a loop guard, keep thinking off for reading tasks, make the DFlash drafter M-RoPE-aware, and add a "zoom" pass that appends a crop of small text to the original image. On speed, the 2B's ~1 s first token is roughly half encoder (506 ms) and a third image prefill (~0.3 s). Resolution budgeting is the only training-free lever that cuts both. f16-accumulate GEMMs, the bias epilogue and an 8-bit projector are worth doing, but only behind a much larger eval set: no published study covers f16 accumulation in a ViT, and the one anecdotal signal of projector-quantization sensitivity is on Qwen3.5 0.8B, larch's own model. Training-free token pruning, the headline speed technique in the literature, is the wrong tool for screenshots. It keeps general VQA intact but cuts OCR and grounding first, and it saves at most ~0.3 s on the 2B because it does not touch the encoder. At larger scale the plan changes. Metal ViT kernels are the biggest missing piece for Macs, and multi-user serving should follow production engines: replicate the ViT rather than shard it, budget encoder work per step, use load-adaptive speculative decoding, and disaggregate the encoder only for image-heavy traffic. The model to standardize on is the Qwen3.5/3.6 family, which covers every tier with one encoder style and one hybrid LM.

How to read the tables. **Tier** is S (small models, 4 GB GPU, CPU fallback), B (bigger GPUs and Macs, single user) or M (multi-user serving). **Type** is E (pure engine work on existing weights), N (support for a new, already-released model) or T (needs training or retraining). **Evidence** is *Strong* (primary measurement on larch's model family or a production engine), *Moderate* (primary measurement on other models or sizes, usually 3B-7B) or *Weak* (anecdote, vendor estimate or my inference). Gains marked "est." are my estimates from larch's own numbers, not published results. I also assume two things not stated in the brief: that larch does not yet snapshot hybrid (GDN plus full-attention) state at image boundaries for reuse across regenerate or edit, and that it has not verified whether its ViT attention materializes the full score matrix. Items 12 and 13 depend on these assumptions.

## The 2B's first second splits between encoder and image prefill

Every speed decision depends on where the first-token time goes. On the RTX 3050, the 2B spends **506 ms encoding** a 1280x720 screenshot. It then prefills about 920 visual tokens at ~3,000 tok/s, which is **~0.3 s**. The encoder is therefore just over half of the ~1 s first token, and the image prefill a third. On the 0.8B the encoder (174 ms) is closer to a third of the first token. This split decides which techniques can help. A GUI-agent study on Qwen3-VL-2B measured the same pattern: LLM-side pruning that kept 98.6% of task performance cut prefill 37.8% but **wall clock only 5.4%**, because the encoder and decode dominate ([Where and How to Prune](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26041)). Pruning after the encoder can save at most the prefill third, while resolution changes and encoder kernels attack the larger half.

On accuracy, the Qwen models already score 12/12 with CER 0.01-0.02. The remaining failure modes (misread small words, loops on "describe in detail" with thinking off, hallucinated details) are each matched by a specific, documented fix. They are not a general capability gap that a new kernel could close. The 12-task set is too small to detect the 1-2 point shifts that precision and quantization changes cause. Benchmark choice also hides damage: the FastV re-analysis found that many standard benchmarks barely move under bad pruning even as TextVQA fell 42% ([Feather the Throttle](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13180)). An audit paper showed that answers can stay correct even when no kept token traces back to the supporting text region ([Auditing spatial provenance](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00077)). A larger, fine-text-heavy eval is therefore item 1, ahead of everything else.

**Sampling and loop control (item 2)** is the cheapest accuracy fix available. The Qwen3.5-0.8B card says the model is "more prone to thinking loops than other Qwen3.5 models". It recommends streaming so that loops can be caught, and gives a non-thinking VL preset of **temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, presence_penalty 1.5** ([Qwen3.5-0.8B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B)). larch's loop on "describe in detail" with thinking off matches this exactly. One precedent argues for checking that the penalty is really applied: Ollama accepted presence and repeat penalties for Qwen3.5 and Qwen3-VL but silently discarded them, so some reported loops came from the runtime ([ollama #14493](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/14493)). The same card warns that higher penalties cause language mixing and "slightly lower performance". Transcription legitimately repeats tokens (table cells, digits), so the penalty should drop to about zero for reading tasks, with the loop detector as the backstop. That split is my design inference, not a tested result.

**Thinking routing (item 3)** has unusually clean evidence on larch's own model. On the 0.8B, turning thinking *on* lowers OCRBench from **79.1 to 74.5** and OmniDocBench from **70.6 to 61.0**. It raises HallusionBench from 46.7 to 53.1 and MMLongBench-Doc from 28.1 to 33.6 ([Qwen3.5-0.8B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B)). Research on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B reasoning variants finds the same pattern: perception peaks at short or moderate reasoning length, visual attention fades as chains grow, and 3B models "decline noticeably" where 7B models stay stable ([More Thinking, Less Seeing?](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21523v2)). The practical rule is: no thinking when the user asks the model to read, and a short budget when the question needs reasoning about what was read. This also gives the "hallucinated details" failure a measured lever, because the HallusionBench gain comes with thinking on.

**The drafter position fix (item 4)** follows from how M-RoPE assigns positions. For image tokens the temporal id is constant and the height and width ids follow the 2D grid. Text after the image resumes at max position + 1 ([Qwen2-VL paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12191v1)). A 1280x720 screenshot becomes a ~40x23 merged grid, so text resumes about 40 positions after the image start, while the KV index has advanced ~920. A drafter that uses the KV index is about 880 positions off for the rest of the chat. Because verification is exact, this costs acceptance rate, not correctness. Uni-LaSpec ran the same model families and shows what correct drafting should deliver. On Qwen3.5-4B at batch 1, a **text-only-trained DFlash reached 2.19x on multimodal prompts** (tau 3.49), against 2.39x for a multimodal-trained DFlash and 0.89x for EAGLE-3. Its drafts use rotary positions of current position plus tree depth ([Uni-LaSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01617)). Expect less headroom on larch's targets: the 0.8B already decodes at 103 tok/s, so the drafter's cost is a larger fraction of each step. ViSpec also measured lower speedups on Qwen-family targets than on LLaVA ([ViSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15235v1)). A quick check is to compare tau with and without an image.

**The 8-bit projector (item 5)** is supported by the literature, with one caveat that points straight at larch. Papers consistently find the vision side less quantization-sensitive than the LM. MBQ measured language-token gradients "over 10x" those of vision tokens and found W4A8 on the ViT caused no significant drop at 7B ([MBQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19509v1)). A January 2026 microscaling benchmark concludes that sensitivity "is dominated by the language model rather than the vision encoder" ([arXiv 2601.09555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09555)). The caveat is a non-scientific Reddit comparison that found Q8 vs F16 mmproj differences insignificant **except on Qwen3.5 0.8B and 4B** ([r/LocalLLaMA](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sgmgtj/snapshots/2026-04-09T16%3A11%3A00.655487Z)). That is weak evidence, but it concerns the exact model larch ships. The 310 MB matters most on the 2B, where it doubles usable context. Ship it per model after item 1 confirms no CER change, starting with the 2B.

**Cache hardening (item 7)** brings larch's content-hash cache up to the production design. vllm-mlx keys images by SHA-256 of decoded pixels, so URL, base64 and file inputs all hit, with a 512 MB LRU ([vllm-mlx paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19139)). SGLang defaults to a 4 GB embedding cache ([LMSYS EPD blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2026-01-12-epd)). vLLM uploads uint8 and runs normalization as one fused per-channel affine kernel, halving PCIe bytes compared with bf16 ([vLLM mm_processing](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/design/mm_processing/)). Each of these takes a few hours. None changes accuracy.

**Preprocessing (item 8)** has one sourced constraint: in the Qwen2-VL report, upscaling small images within a range helped OCRBench and InfoVQA, but an overly high `min_pixels` caused a "severe" OCRBench drop because enlarged tiny images fall out of distribution ([Qwen2-VL report](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12191v1)). EXIF rotation and the HiDPI filter choice are correctness hygiene, with no benchmark behind them.

**f16 accumulation (item 9)** goes beyond what any engine ships. Production engines run the ViT with 16-bit inputs and fp32 accumulation. The vLLM FP8 baseline and the EPD paper both run BF16/FP16 encoders ([vLLM FP8 ViT doc](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/fp8_vit_attn/); [EPD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05460v4)). larch's fp32-accumulate encoder already sits at or above that bar. **No source reports an accuracy study of fp16 accumulation in a ViT.** The speedup is real on GeForce parts, where larch estimates ~2x on the matmuls, but the risk is unmeasured, and the long reductions (MLP down-projection over the 4x-wide hidden layer) are where fp16 sums lose bits. Accumulate the short-K projections in fp16 and keep the rest in fp32. Then compare per-layer embedding cosine against the f64 reference, plus CER on the expanded eval. This is "next" rather than "now" only because it needs item 1 first.

**The zoom pass (item 10)** is the strongest accuracy addition for "misread small words", and it works on frozen weights. ViCrop chooses a crop from the model's own attention for the first answer token and appends the crop's tokens to the original image, so the model keeps the global view. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, TextVQA rose **47.80 → 55.17** with no regressions on general VQA ([ViCrop](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17422v1)). A third-party run on **Qwen2.5-VL-3B** gives the small-model number: TextVQA **68.20 → 72.91-74.15**, DocVQA 69.52 → 71.40-72.27, InfoVQA 44.03 → 47.94-49.43 ([CropVLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19820v2)). That run used 448 px inputs, so gains at larch's 1,600-token cap will be smaller. ViCrop's published cost (~1 s, 19 GB on an A6000) comes from materializing attention or gradients. larch can avoid most of it: the method needs only the first answer token's attention to image keys, which is one query row per head in the full-attention layers. larch can compute that row from cached K outside the flash kernel. A simpler fallback is a deterministic crop of the densest small-text region. Trigger the pass only for reading questions, which also makes it a natural place for a per-line vote between the original and the zoomed read. No source tests that vote.

**Resolution tiers (item 11)** are the only training-free lever that cuts encoder and prefill time together. larch's 1,600-token cap sits near the OCRBench sweet spot in the Qwen2-VL-7B ablation (824 at 1,600 tokens vs 786 at 3,136). Dense-document InfoVQA keeps improving with more tokens (74.99 → 77.27), and MMMU stays flat at every budget ([Qwen2-VL report](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12191v1)). So general and reasoning questions can run at lower resolution cheaply, and reading questions cannot. The GUI study measured mild downscaling (x0.8) at **-1.0% relative** on Qwen3-VL-2B navigation, less than removing 36% of patches before the ViT (-2.5%) ([Where and How to Prune](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26041)). The keyword- or intent-based tiering itself is untested; trained per-query selectors (CARES, GlimpsePrune) appear under research bets.

**Tiled ViT attention (item 12)** is mostly a memory fix on a 4 GB card. With 3,680 patches, one layer's full score matrix is ~13.5 M entries per head. At f32 across all heads it approaches a gigabyte if materialized at once (est.). When llama.cpp turned on flash attention in its clip encoder, the Metal compute buffer fell from **1,132 MiB to 121 MiB** and per-image encode time fell 16-26% ([llama.cpp PR #16837](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16837)). A later llama.cpp issue hit an INT_MAX copy limit on large images from FA masks and fixed it by chunking Q ([llama.cpp #17801](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/17801)). The PR page also links a report of CUDA FA producing wrong image embeddings on Qwen3.5/Qwen3-VL. The research could not confirm it, but it is a reason to validate against the f64 reference.

**Hybrid-state snapshots (item 13)** are the multi-turn win larch does not yet have, under my assumption above. vllm-mlx's ablation on Qwen3-VL-8B (M4 Max, 1024x1024) shows uncached turns at 21.7 s, embedding reuse alone at 2.8 s (7.8x), KV reuse alone at 18.2 s, and both together at **1.15 s (19x)**, falling to 0.78 s from turn three ([vllm-mlx paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19139)). larch already has the embedding half. The other half is harder for a hybrid model, because GDN state is sequential and can only be resumed from a saved checkpoint. vLLM's first attempt hit 0% prefix-cache hits in "align" mode. Its working "all" mode snapshots GDN SSM and conv states at block boundaries **in float32**, because a bf16 round trip gave a max diff of 2.0 in kernel tests ([vLLM PR #36649](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/36649)). For larch, snapshotting once after each image span and once per completed turn keeps the design simple. Key the snapshots on the image hash, as the Tenstorrent design note does with multimodal block hashes ([Tenstorrent #270](https://github.com/tenstorrent/vllm-tt-plugin/issues/270)).

**An OCR side channel (item 15)** helps exactly the models that read worst. Adding OCR text to the prompt lifted InternVL2.5-7B from **87 to 162** on a 250-question Korean benchmark, but Qwen2.5-VL-7B only from 198 to 212. A weaker OCR engine shrank both gains ([OCR-augmented generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02543)). At ImageCLEF 2026, OCR injection did not help Qwen3-VL at all ([FAU at ImageCLEF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01664)). The fit for larch is therefore model-dependent. It is worth it for InternVL (CER 0.05-0.11) and for pages whose text exceeds what the 1,600-token cap resolves. It is not worth it as a default for Qwen3.5 (CER 0.01-0.02). PaddleOCR-VL is the strongest candidate. It is 0.9B, has an official GGUF and was merged into llama.cpp in February 2026 ([llama.cpp multimodal.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)). Its 1.6 version scores 96.34 on OmniDocBench v1.6, a secondary figure ([Docsumo](https://www.docsumo.com/blog/best-ocr-models)). It needs fixed prompts, so expose it as a tool ("transcribe this page"), not a chat model. A transcript in the chat also gives prompt-lookup decoding text to copy, which it cannot get from image tokens.

**Metal (item 16)** is a feature gap, not an optimization: images are off on macOS. The port order from llama.cpp's experience is fused attention first, mainly for memory, since a 9x smaller compute buffer matters on 8-16 GB unified-memory Macs ([llama.cpp PR #16837](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16837)). The competitive bar from vllm-mlx is **0.8-2.1 s cold** (encode plus prefill) for Qwen3-VL-4B at 224²-1024² on an M4 Max. The same paper measured embedding-plus-KV caching cutting later turns to ~0.15 s ([vllm-mlx paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19139)). FastVLM, measured on an M1, confirms that the vision encoder dominates TTFT at high resolution on Apple hardware ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/fast-vision-language-models)).

**The CPU path (item 17)** has no good published benchmark, only negative evidence. llama.cpp calls CPU mmproj encoding "slow", and one llama-server run took **82 s per image** with the encoder silently on CPU in BF16 ([llama.cpp #17801](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/17801); [llama.cpp #22582](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/22582)). With full attention over thousands of patches, kernel tuning alone will not bring CPU encode under the step watchdog. Cut the patch count hard, accept the accuracy cost, and slice the encode across scheduler steps as larch already does on GPU.

**CUDA graphs for the ViT (item 18)** suit batch 1 in principle; vLLM's own design doc says "small batches gain the most". But its measured gains are milliseconds on 5 ms encodes (GB200: 5.13 → 4.52 ms mean) ([vLLM CUDA graphs multimodal](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/design/cuda_graphs_multimodal/)). For a 24-layer ViT taking 174-506 ms, launch overhead is probably a small share. Profile the gaps between kernels first. If they matter, copy vLLM's scheme: about 36 padded token budgets with shared capture buffers, so memory stays near one graph's worth.

**A visual-aware drafter (item 19)** is the follow-up once positions are fixed. In Uni-LaSpec, attending to about **eight verifier-selected visual tokens** kept at least 98.6% of full-image acceptance. The gains from visual information concentrated in the document/OCR group (Gemma 4 E2B tau 2.35 → 2.79 → 3.01) ([Uni-LaSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01617)). GLANCE drafts a block in one pass over the target's fused hidden states and reports up to 3.05x, with its longest accepted blocks on grounded, low-entropy tasks ([GLANCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00355)). Transcription is exactly that kind of task. Both need training, so this item lands after the free fix in item 4.

**Image KV eviction (item 21)** is worth less on larch's default model than the literature suggests. VL-Cache keeps ~10% of KV at near-full accuracy ([VL-Cache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23317)), and LOOK-M approaches full-cache performance at 5% on LLaVA-7B ([LOOK-M](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18139)). Both were measured on dense models. The Qwen3.5-2B has 6 full-attention layers with 2 KV heads of dim 256 ([Qwen3.5-2B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B)). That works out to about **12 KiB per token** at fp16, roughly 9x less than InternVL3.5-1B's ~112 KiB. A 920-token screenshot costs about 11 MB of KV on the 2B. Eviction pays only in long multi-image chats, and it cannot touch what the GDN layers already folded into their state.

**The serving items (22-28)** follow where production engines have converged. Small ViTs should be replicated, not sharded. SGLang found Qwen2.5-VL-72B's ViT no faster at TP8 than TP2 (524 vs 492 ms) ([LMSYS EPD blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2026-01-12-epd)). vLLM's data-parallel encoder mode raised mean throughput by +6.2% (Qwen3-VL-235B) to +17.7% (InternVL3.5-241B), and tensor-parallel all-reduces had taken ~40% of one model's encoder time ([AMD ROCm blog](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/vllm-dp-vision/README.html)). vLLM rejects encoder work beyond a per-step `encoder_compute_budget` ([vLLM encoder cache manager](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.11.2/api/vllm/v1/core/encoder_cache_manager/)). That is the multi-user generalization of larch's layer-sliced encode. Disaggregation pays only for image-heavy traffic. The EPD paper reports up to **71.9% lower TTFT** and 15x lower peak memory ([EPD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05460v4)), and ModServe 3.3-5.5x throughput on production traces ([ModServe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00937)). SGLang explicitly calls it "counterproductive" for image-light workloads. Speculative decoding needs a load policy: at concurrency 64, fixed-block drafting on Qwen3.5-4B fell to **0.82x** on multimodal prompts, and MTP heads on a Qwen3.5-2B to 0.54x. A load-adaptive policy stayed above 1x in every setting tested ([Uni-LaSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01617)). FP8 ViT attention is near-lossless (ChartQA relaxed accuracy 0.780 vs 0.780 static) but **slower below FullHD** ([vLLM FP8 ViT doc](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/fp8_vit_attn/)). LM quantization at scale should stay weight-only or 8-bit. MBQ shows W4A8 is where OCR breaks (OCRBench 62.2 → 52.3, TextVQA 76.1 → 68.3 on LLaVA-onevision-7B), and modality-balanced calibration recovers much of it ([MBQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19509v1)). For video, EVS halved TTFT at 75% pruning on Nemotron Nano V2 VL ([Nemotron Nano V2 VL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03929)). vLLM's Qwen3-VL integration shows the trap: the pruned token count is known only after encoding, which breaks pre-laid-out placeholders ([vLLM #30847](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/30847)).

One quantization check belongs on the 4 GB tier too. Official Qwen2-VL-2B numbers show 4-bit AWQ costing **1.4 DocVQA points** (88.34 → 86.96) ([Qwen quantization benchmark](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started/quantization_benchmark.html)). Small models have less redundancy than the 7B-72B models most papers test. The 2B's Q4_K_M OCR cost should therefore be measured on the expanded eval, not assumed. Use the result to decide whether 8 GB users get Q6_K.

**Pruning (bet 29)** is a bet rather than a plan because the evidence turns against it exactly on text. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, VisionZip keeps TextVQA at 56.8 with 128 of 576 tokens, against 50.6 for FastV ([VisionZip](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04467)). On modern models, an October 2026 study finds training-free pruning "largely preserves coarse-grained understanding but sharply degrades OCR and grounding". Even the best projector-side methods (DivPrune, AIM) retain only ~94% of OCR at 40% keep and ~60-64% at 10% ([Rescuing Your Pruning](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33688)). Text-aware pruning on Qwen3-VL-8B at 80% still loses **16.6 points on InfoVQA** ([When Text Matters](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34861v1)). Stronger models draw on visual tokens deeper into the LM, and every method retains less on Qwen2.5-VL than on LLaVA ([arXiv 2512.07580](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07580v1); [ResPrune](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21105)). On Qwen3-VL-2B GUI tasks at 50%, random sampling (0.7027) matched or beat every importance method ([Where and How to Prune](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26041)). If larch tries pruning at all, use spatially uniform or diversity selection after the merger, only for description prompts. Gather each survivor's original (t, h, w) ids and leave text positions at full-grid max + 1, which needs no RoPE kernel change. vLLM's PR #38888 is an engine-level reference for M-RoPE reattachment ([vLLM PR #38888](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/38888)).

**The trained bets (30-35)** produce the largest accuracy jumps in the notes, which is why they stay on the list. A 256M learned cropper lifted Qwen2.5-VL-3B's average **56.42 → 67.14** at 1.2-1.7 GB peak memory ([CropVLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19820v2)). GlimpsePrune's trained predictor kept DocVQA at 0.962 using **3.6% of tokens** on Qwen2.5-VL-7B ([GlimpsePrune](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01548v1)). CARES claims up to 80% compute reduction by predicting the minimal sufficient resolution ([CARES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19496)). InternVL3.5-Flash halves tokens, but its 4.05x headline mixes in decoupled serving, and no 1B/2B Flash OCR table was found ([InternVL3.5 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18265v2)). Fine-tuning delivers less than model swaps. A Qwen3.5-2B student gained +5.8 on ScreenSpot-V2 and +3.5 on ScreenSpot-Pro from distillation plus RL ([WinDOM](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67777)).

**Skip these.** Each conflicts with larch's workload or hardware:

## Model choices: one Qwen3.5/3.6 code path covers 4 GB to 64 GB

The Qwen3.5 family is the right default for screenshots at every tier. It also lets one encoder style and one hybrid LM code path serve every tier. On a 4 GB card, **Qwen3.5-2B** matches InternVL3.5-2B on OCRBench (84.4 vs 83.9 when Liquid ran both) and leads on DocVQA (92.6 vs 88.4) and InfoVQA (73.5 vs 69.3) ([Liquid LFM2.5-VL blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/lfm2-5-vl-3b)). On ScreenSpot-Pro it scores 54.5 by its own card ([Qwen3.5-2B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B)), against ~21 for InternVL3.5-4B in Liquid's table ([LFM3-VL-2B card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-VL-2B)). It also uses about 9x less KV per token. InternVL3.5-2B leads on ScreenSpot-v2 desktop (79.9 vs 63.8) and ChartQA in the same Liquid run. The ScreenSpot conflict probably reflects prompt or coordinate formats, so test both on larch's own UI set before demoting InternVL entirely. InternVL3.5-1B (OCRBench 795, DocVQA 85.6) is the weakest reader larch ships ([InternVL3.5 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18265)). Pair it with the OCR tool or retire it.

Two newcomers deserve tracking rather than immediate ports. **LFM3-VL-2B** posts vendor numbers above Qwen3.5-2B on OCRBench (86.3 vs 83.6) and ScreenSpot-Pro (55.5 vs 38.1 in Liquid's run). It is a new conv/attention encoder plus a hybrid conv LM, though, so it means new kernels throughout, and no independent numbers exist yet ([LFM3-VL-2B card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-VL-2B)). **MiniCPM-V 4.6** is the cheaper experiment, because its text model is the Qwen3.5-0.8B larch already runs. Skip SmolVLM2, Moondream, Gemma 3 and, for OCR work, Gemma 4 E2B/E4B (OCRBench ~72) ([LFM3-VL-2B card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-VL-2B)).

## Conclusion

The literature's dominant efficiency idea, dropping visual tokens, targets a cost larch barely has. On a 4 GB GPU the bottleneck is the encoder, and the accuracy problem is reading small text. Pruning does nothing for the first and actively hurts the second. larch's best moves run the other way: spend more tokens selectively (a zoom crop on reading questions), fewer tokens selectively (lower resolution on description questions), and none twice (hash-keyed embeddings plus fp32 hybrid-state snapshots). Applied by task type, these give both accuracy and speed gains without retraining. The same task signal, reading versus reasoning versus describing, also sets the thinking mode, the presence penalty and the OCR tool call. One cheap prompt classifier therefore drives most of the "do now" and "next" gains.

The main open risk is measurement, not engineering. Nothing published says whether fp16 accumulation is safe in a ViT, small models have the least redundancy to absorb error, and the one sensitivity signal points at larch's 0.8B. larch's f64-referenced encoder is its quiet advantage: no other engine can as easily check each precision change per layer against ground truth. Paired with a few hundred fine-text screenshots, it turns the speed items (f16 GEMMs, 8-bit projector, Q4 vs Q6 LM) from guesses into measured decisions. Past 4 GB, the Qwen3.5/3.6 family means the Metal port, hybrid-state caching and load-adaptive drafting each have to be built only once to cover every tier from 0.8B to 35B-A3B.
